# Machine Learning: An Artificial Intelligence Approach, Vol. I

> [!info] ? · 1983 · Tioga

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the publisher description / catalog listing — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — The landmark edited volume (Michalski, Carbonell & Mitchell, eds.) that helped consolidate machine learning as a distinct subfield of AI, collecting tutorial overviews and research papers on symbolic, knowledge-based learning.
**Problem** — In the early 1980s, work on learning was scattered; the volume asks why and how machines should learn and organizes the emerging approaches.
**Method** — A ~572-page collection organized into six parts, opening with an overview of machine learning (why machines should learn) and covering issues in designing learning programs, notably learning from examples, in the symbolic-AI tradition of the era.
**Key results** — An edited collection rather than a single result; influential in defining the agenda and vocabulary of early machine learning.

## Takeaways
- Foundational reference for the symbolic / knowledge-intensive era of machine learning (concept learning, learning from examples) that predates the statistical-learning dominance.
- Frames learning as an AI problem, complementing the statistical view of works like [[@duda2000pattern]].
- Cited to anchor the historical and conceptual roots of "learning" in AI.
