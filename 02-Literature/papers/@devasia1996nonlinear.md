# Nonlinear inversion-based output tracking

> [!info] Devasia, S.; Degang Chen; Paden, B. · 1996 · IEEE TAC

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the abstract — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — A stable inversion procedure that constructs a bounded input producing exact tracking of a desired output for nonlinear systems, including nonminimum-phase ones.
**Problem** — For nonminimum-phase nonlinear systems, naive dynamic inversion yields unstable (unbounded) input trajectories, so exact output tracking is not achievable by causal inversion alone.
**Method** — An inversion procedure builds a bounded input trajectory in the preimage of the desired output; for minimum-phase systems it recovers Hirschorn's inverse, but for nonminimum-phase systems the preimage trajectory is noncausal (preactuation) rather than unstable. The inverse is computed by a Picard-like iteration instead of solving a regulator PDE, and stabilizing feedback added to the preimage trajectory yields asymptotically exact tracking.
**Key results** — Establishes a geometric link between the unstable manifold of the zero dynamics and the noncausality of the inverse; demonstrates tracking on a numerical example and compares against the Byrnes-Isidori regulator. Truncating the noncausal input when preactuation is impossible reproduces the tracking-error transients of other schemes.

## Takeaways
- The core trade for nonminimum-phase systems is noncausality (require preactuation / knowledge of the future reference), not instability — a cleaner way to think about the limits of output tracking.
- Avoids solving the regulator PDE by iterating for the inverse, which is computationally attractive.
- Zero-dynamics geometry directly governs how much preview/preactuation you need; truncation trades exactness for realizability.

## Relevance to your work
Foundational output-tracking / dynamic-inversion result grounding constructive nonlinear-control approaches; the zero-dynamics-and-noncausality picture is exactly the machinery invoked when designing tracking controllers for underactuated locomotion. See [[@compton2024constructive]].

## Abstract (from bib)
An inversion procedure is introduced for nonlinear systems which constructs a bounded input trajectory in the preimage of a desired output trajectory. In the case of minimum phase systems, the trajectory produced agrees with that generated by Hirschorn’s inverse dynamic system; however, the preimage trajectory is noncausal (rather than unstable) in the nonminimum phase case. In addition, the analysis leads to a simple geometric connection between the unstable manifold of the system zero dynamics and noncausality in the nonminimum phase case. With the addition of stabilizing feedback to the preimage trajectory, asymptotically exact output tracking is achieved.
