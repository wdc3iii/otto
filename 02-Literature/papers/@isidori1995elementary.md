# Elementary Theory of Nonlinear Feedback for Single-Input Single-Output Systems

> [!info] Isidori, Alberto · 1995 · Nonlinear Control Systems

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the chapter abstract/preface — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — The opening chapter of Isidori's geometric-nonlinear-control development, laying out the elementary feedback theory for single-input single-output (SISO) nonlinear systems before the multivariable and advanced tools of later chapters.
**Problem** — How to systematically synthesize feedback control laws for nonlinear systems, starting with the tractable SISO case whose structure admits an elementary analysis.
**Method** — Treats the SISO nonlinear system as the base case in an increasing-complexity program: exploits the simple input-output structure (relative degree, normal form, zero dynamics) to build feedback laws, then flags how the theory extends to a special class of multivariable systems and, later, to more general nonlinear systems.
**Key results** — A self-contained foundation for input-output/exact feedback linearization and zero-dynamics analysis of SISO nonlinear plants; the springboard for the more powerful design tools developed in subsequent chapters.

## Takeaways
- Canonical reference for the SISO nonlinear-control machinery: relative degree, normal form, and zero dynamics.
- Framed as the elementary case; the deeper results (multivariable extensions, general synthesis) are deferred to later chapters, so this is foundational scaffolding rather than a standalone result.
- Textbook chapter, not a research article — cited for its formalism, not novel findings.

## Abstract (from bib)
Beginning with this Chapter, we will study — in order of increasing complexity — a series of problems concerned with the synthesis of feedback control laws for nonlinear systems of the form (1.2). We will discuss first the case of single-input single-output systems, whose simple structure lends itself to a rather elementary analysis, and then — in the next Chapter — a special class of multivariable systems, in which a straightforward extension of most of the theory developed for single-input single-output systems is possible Finally — in the last four Chapters — we will present a set of more powerful tools for the analysis and the design of more general classes of nonlinear control systems.
