# Output regulation of nonlinear systems

> [!info] Isidori, A.; Byrnes, C.I. · 1990 · IEEE TAC

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the abstract — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — Establishes the nonlinear output-regulation theory: a fixed nonlinear plant can be made to asymptotically track references (and reject disturbances) generated by an exosystem iff a certain nonlinear PDE (the regulator equations) is solvable.
**Problem** — Extend the linear output-regulation / internal-model results to nonlinear plants: when can feedback force a plant's output to track/reject a family of signals produced by an external generator?
**Method** — Under standard assumptions, reduces solvability of the regulation problem to the existence of a solution of a nonlinear partial differential equation (the Francis-Byrnes-Isidori regulator equations); given such a solution, a stabilizing feedback law that solves the problem is constructed directly.
**Key results** — A necessary-and-sufficient solvability condition for nonlinear output regulation that specializes to and generalizes the classical linear-systems results.

## Takeaways
- Foundational result: nonlinear servomechanism / internal-model theory reduces to the solvability of the regulator PDEs.
- The condition is existence of a center-manifold-like invariant map; constructing it explicitly is the hard part in practice.
- Bridges linear internal-model control to the nonlinear geometric-control setting.

## Relevance to your work
Output regulation is the general form of the tracking problem that certificate- and linearization-based controllers solve locally; it grounds the tracking guarantees invoked in [[@compton2024constructive]].
