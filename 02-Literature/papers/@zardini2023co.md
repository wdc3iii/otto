# Co-design of complex systems: From autonomy to future mobility systems

> [!info] Zardini, Gioele · 2023 · —

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the dissertation description (ETH Zurich PhD thesis, Frazzoli group) — a base to refine. Full text not fetched; confirm details against the source.
**TL;DR** — A monotone/compositional theory of *co-design* for complex engineered systems, applied from single-robot autonomy up to future mobility systems, so that coupled design choices across components and abstraction levels can be optimized jointly.
**Problem** — Designing complex systems (e.g., autonomous vehicles and the mobility systems they enable) involves many interacting trade-offs across components, scales, and abstraction levels; choosing components in isolation misses their coupling, and there is a need for a principled, scalable framework to reason about these joint trade-offs.
**Method** — Develops a mathematical co-design framework (building on monotone theory of co-design / design problems over ordered resource–functionality relations) that composes individual design problems into system-level ones, enabling computation of Pareto-optimal trade-off frontiers; instantiates it across levels from robot autonomy to transportation/mobility-system design and policy.
**Key results** — A compositional, scalable methodology (with supporting algorithms) linking technical component design to system- and policy-level mobility outcomes; a unifying "from autonomy to future mobility" perspective rather than a single empirical result.

## Takeaways
- Frames engineering as *co-design*: components and the system they serve are coupled and should be optimized together, not sequentially.
- Compositionality is the scalability lever — local design problems compose into system-level trade-off analyses.
- A systems/methodology thesis; specifics of theorems and case studies should be verified against the full document.
