# Reinforcement Learning-Based Footstep Control for Humanoid Robots on Complex Terrain

> [!info] Suliman, William; Davydenko, Egor; Chaikovskaia, Ekaterina; Gorbachev, Roman · 2025 · IEEE Access

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the abstract — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — A modular RL footstep-control framework where a learned step-following policy executes commands (position, orientation, height) from a heuristic, model-free planner, giving stable bipedal locomotion over complex terrain.
**Problem** — Enabling humanoids to walk stably over challenging, non-flat terrain while remaining interpretable and safe, without relying on dynamic models or opaque end-to-end perception-to-torque mappings.
**Method** — A heuristic planner maps user velocity/orientation inputs plus a local height field into adaptive footstep commands (placement and height); a learned low-level policy tracks these using two-step-per-leg foresight. Perception is decoupled from control in a modular architecture, and the planner uses no dynamic model.
**Key results** — A foresight-horizon study (one/two/three-step) finds two-step gives the best performance-complexity trade-off; the robot clears obstacles up to 50% of leg length and tracks speeds up to 0.8 m/s, validated in simulation on the kid-sized Bruce humanoid, competitive with a model-based baseline.

## Takeaways
- Model-free heuristic planning + learned tracker matches a model-based approach without needing explicit dynamics.
- Modular perception/control decoupling is argued to improve interpretability, debuggability, and safety (low-level policy always gets feasible commands).
- Two-step foresight again emerges as the sweet spot (cf. [[@singh2022learning]]).

## Abstract (from bib)
We propose a Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based footstep planning framework that enables a bipedal robot to achieve stable locomotion over challenging terrain. At its core, a learned step-following policy executes footstep commands with varying positions, orientations, and heights. These commands are generated by a heuristic planner that processes user-defined velocity and orientation inputs alongside a local height field to adaptively adjust step placement and height. By leveraging two-step foresight commands for each leg, the controller ensures safe and adaptive locomotion in dynamic environments. A comparative study of different foresight horizons (one-step, two-step, and three-step) demonstrates that the two-step controller provides the best trade-off between performance and complexity.
