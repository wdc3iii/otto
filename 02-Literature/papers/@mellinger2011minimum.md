# Minimum snap trajectory generation and control for quadrotors

> [!info] D. Mellinger; V. Kumar · 2011 · ICRA

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the abstract — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — An algorithm that generates optimal minimum-snap quadrotor trajectories through 3-D position/yaw waypoints subject to corridor and dynamic-limit constraints, paired with a nonlinear tracking controller for aggressive indoor flight.
**Problem** — Flying quadrotors through tightly constrained indoor spaces requires large attitude excursions where small-angle approximations for roll and pitch fail, and trajectories must respect corridors and velocity/acceleration/input bounds in real time.
**Method** — Trajectories are generated by minimizing snap (the fourth derivative of position) over a sequence of 3-D positions and yaw angles, formulated so that safe passage through specified corridors and constraints on velocities, accelerations, and inputs are enforced, and solved fast enough for real-time use. A nonlinear controller then tracks the resulting trajectories faithfully.
**Key results** — Experimental demonstrations of fast flight (roughly 5-10 body lengths per second) through three-dimensional slalom courses.

## Takeaways
- Minimizing snap is motivated by the quadrotor's differential flatness: snap maps directly to motor commands, so smooth snap means feasible, aggressive trajectories.
- Corridor and actuator constraints are baked into the trajectory optimization, not patched on afterward.
- A foundational planning-layer method: it produces the reference trajectory that a full-order attitude/thrust controller then tracks.

## Relevance to your work
A canonical instance of the plan-then-track split — optimize a smooth reference on a flat/reduced representation, then track it with a nonlinear inner loop — the layered pattern that safety-critical and reduced-order work such as [[@cohen2025safety]] generalizes to legged systems.
