# Pattern Classification

> [!info] R. O. Duda; P. E. Hart; D. G. Stork · 2000 · John Wiley and Sons

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the publisher description / table of contents — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — The standard graduate textbook on statistical pattern recognition, organizing classification, estimation, and clustering within a Bayesian decision-theoretic framework.
**Problem** — A unified, teachable treatment of how to build systems that classify patterns from data.
**Method** — Builds from Bayesian decision theory through maximum-likelihood and Bayesian parameter estimation, nonparametric techniques, linear discriminant functions, multilayer neural networks, stochastic methods, nonmetric methods, algorithm-independent machine learning, and unsupervised learning and clustering. The 2nd edition adds substantial material on neural networks, statistical pattern recognition, learning theory, and invariances.
**Key results** — Reference textbook rather than a results paper; widely used as the canonical source for supervised/unsupervised classification methods.

## Takeaways
- Bayesian decision theory is the organizing spine: classifiers are analyzed as decisions minimizing expected loss.
- Spans the parametric–nonparametric and supervised–unsupervised axes, giving a common vocabulary for classification and clustering.
- A default citation for foundational pattern-recognition / supervised-learning concepts.
