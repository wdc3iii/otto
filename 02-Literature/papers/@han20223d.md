# 3D-SLIP model based dynamic stability strategy for legged robots with impact disturbance rejection

> [!info] Han, Bin; Yi, Haoyuan; Xu, Zhenyu; Yang, Xin; Luo, Xin · 2022 · Scientific Reports

## Summary
> [!note] AI-drafted from the abstract — a base to refine.
**TL;DR** — A Raibert-style control strategy on a 3D spring-loaded inverted pendulum (SLIP) template lets legged robots reject lateral impact disturbances while running.

**Problem** — The classic SLIP model captures sagittal-plane running, but lateral external disturbances force motion out of the 2-D plane; extending to full 3-D raises dimensionality and makes real-time control hard, especially with a full-order leg model.

**Method** — The authors build a strategy (named 3D-HFC) on top of the classical Raibert controller with three modules: touchdown-angle control, body-attitude-angle control, and energy compensation. Per-step energy loss is measured online and used to predict and compensate the next step's energy, keeping the running gait bounded under impacts.

**Key results** — Demonstrated in simulation on a 3D-SLIP reduced model and a simulated running quadruped under varying impact forces, and validated on a physical quadruped prototype (MBBOT) in lateral-impact experiments; both show successful impact-disturbance rejection.

## Takeaways
- Uses the SLIP template as the design abstraction, then maps a Raibert-style heuristic onto it — a classic template/anchor approach rather than optimization-based control.
- Online per-step energy estimation drives the energy-compensation module; the method is heuristic (no formal stability guarantee stated).
- Validated in both simulation and hardware, but on a quadruped prototype, and framed around running/hopping dynamics.

## Relevance to your work
A template-level (reduced-order) treatment of disturbance rejection in legged locomotion, relevant background for anyone framing locomotion around a simple model and then asking for robustness margins to impacts — contrast its heuristic energy compensation with the formal robustness guarantees pursued in [[@csomayshanklin2024robust]].
