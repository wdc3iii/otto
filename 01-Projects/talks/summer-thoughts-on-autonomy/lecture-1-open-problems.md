# Lecture 1 — Open Problems in Autonomy

Part of [[summer-thoughts-on-autonomy|Summer Thoughts on Autonomy]] · recorded 2026-06-19 ·
[watch](https://youtu.be/dViOABt4Eyk)

## Summary (from the lecture description)
A framework for thinking about problems in autonomy along two axes — **Structure** and
**Stakes** — together with three distinct senses in which a problem can be considered
"solved": **theoretically**, **practically**, and **domain-specifically**. The lecture maps
common solution techniques onto these axes and draws out what the picture implies for a
young researcher deciding where to work.

## Key ideas
- **Two axes:** Structure (how much structure the problem admits) × Stakes (cost of failure).
- **Three notions of "solved":** theoretical guarantees vs. practical performance vs.
  domain-specific sufficiency — different bars for different problems.
- Positioning of solution techniques (classical control, learning, hybrids) against these
  axes, and the strategic takeaway for early-career researchers.
